# Changelog

Every release of [oregonwine.app](https://www.oregonwine.app), newest first, in the words of the site's own "What's new". Versions before 1.0 were not numbered; they are summarised by day at the end.

## 1.56 · 30 October 2026

**The biggest gain in data yet: over a thousand more people and places from the interviews, and a calmer graph**

- About 1,400 more people and places and 3,600 more connections, drawn from the interviews themselves: tasting-room staff, interns, assistants and the careers that ran through them.
- The graph opens calmer: the best-connected people and places are drawn in full and the rest faintly, with their lines and names appearing as you zoom in. Opening any record draws it, and everything it connects to, in full.
- Dozens of duplicates merged into one record each, under the spelling their owners use, such as Ian Burch, Russ Rosner and Briana Seeley.
- Forty more vineyards on the map, from King Estate and Amalie Robert to Cristom's named blocks, and new records for Balcombe, Corrine, Lia's and La Belle Promenade vineyards.
- Opening a record on the map now zooms in to it; a button over the map, or the Z key, shows all its connections.
- A link copied from the map or the list now opens in the same view.

## 1.55 · 29 October 2026

**The map follows the years, shows where the vines are, and remembers old names**

- Go back in time now works on the map: wineries and vineyards appear as they were founded or planted, and AVAs as they were recognised, from Willamette Valley in 1983.
- Over a hundred vineyards are now on the map, up from thirteen, most at their own site and some placed roughly from how sources describe them; the land under vines, as mapped on OpenStreetMap, shows faintly green.
- Hover a vineyard's outline to see whose it is, or click it to open it.
- Renamed wineries and vineyards show their former names, such as Sidereus, formerly Blooming Hill, and on the map they go by the name they had in the year chosen.
- With a winery or vineyard open, Zoom to it over the map (or press Z) to see it close up, and again to see all its connections.
- The zoom buttons now sit at the bottom right, as on a map, at every screen size, and the selection's vineyards glow so they stand out.

## 1.54 · 29 October 2026

**The vineyards a winery buys from, on the map**

- Opening a winery on the map now shows the vineyards it buys from, and opening a vineyard all the wineries it supplies, framed together. A vineyard whose exact place isn't on record appears faintly inside its AVA, with a dotted line.
- Fruit sourcing on a card has a new link: See its vineyards on the map, or on a vineyard, See the wineries it supplies on the map.
- Thirteen vineyards now have a place of their own, including Temperance Hill, Nysa, Hyland, Ridgecrest and Saffron Fields.
- On the map, the open record and its connections always sit on top, their lines stand out more, and everything else is fainter.
- Highland Vineyard was Hyland Vineyard heard wrong; the two are now one record.
- Ways to explore now describes the three views right after Search.

## 1.53 · 29 October 2026

**392 wineries on the map, the first vineyards, and where the fruit goes**

- Eighty-seven more wineries on the map: 21 newly found tasting-room addresses, and 66 small labels placed where their federal permit says their wine is made, with a dashed ring and “Made at” on their card.
- Labels made at one shared winery, such as Carlton Winemakers Studio, now gather around it on the map, and hovering one names the others made there.
- The first vineyards on the map, outlined from OpenStreetMap: Shea, Corral Creek, Roserock, Antica Terra, Seven Hills and Watermill. Opening one draws a line to each winery on the map that buys its fruit.
- New people: Dago Guillén, tasting room manager at White Rose since 2013, and Griffin Taylor, who runs hospitality at Rodeo Hills.

## 1.25 · 19 October 2026

**Label approvals and WineEnthusiast reviews as sources; years shown as “1965 or 1966”**

- Federal label approvals (TTB’s COLA registry) are a source: they name the vineyards and AVAs on a winery’s labels. A connection resting only on approvals says so. The applicant’s contact details are never kept.
- WineEnthusiast reviews from 2017 are a source for facts only: winery, wine name, region and grape, never the reviewer’s words, scores or prices.
- The automated check reads a summary sentence by sentence, reads PDFs, and uses the Internet Archive’s copy of pages it can’t reach.
- Research on 200 draft records added 283 sources and about 58 corrections, each logged with its source.
- Where sources disagree on a year, the record reads “1965 or 1966”, with an “i” that explains.
